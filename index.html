Experiment-constrained ML for atomistic science

We build ML potentials for materials where local models alone are not enough.

The Ganesh Sivaraman Lab develops active-learning workflows, long-range MLIP corrections, and experiment-validated potentials, then applies them to beamline-guided studies of disordered oxides, layered chalcogenides, and data-driven biomedical materials discovery with UB School of Dental Medicine collaborators.

What makes the work distinct

Methods are built around validation, not just fitting.

Our work asks a practical question: can a computer model stay anchored to what experiments actually see while helping guide useful materials choices? We combine beamline measurements, quantum calculations, active learning, and molecular simulation for technologically relevant disordered oxides and layered chalcogenides, while also applying the same methodological foundation with biomedical collaborators to support rigorous, application-facing discovery.

1Measure

X-ray, neutron, or molecular data reveal structure under realistic conditions.

2Learn

Active learning selects the most informative configurations for expensive calculations.

3Validate

Simulations are checked against measured structure before being used for prediction.
