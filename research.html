Research program

Machine learning that connects atoms, experiments, and materials design.

Our research program develops physically grounded machine-learning models and uses them to interpret real materials and molecular systems.

Research Thrusts

Thrust 1

Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials with Long-Range Physics

Automated active-learning workflows for MLIP training, reciprocal-space corrections, and robust simulations beyond short-range local environments.

Thrust 2

AI-Guided Discovery of Safer, High-Performance Biomedical Monomers

Computational-experimental co-design with molecular machine learning, uncertainty-aware screening, and targeted validation.

Thrust 3

MLIP-Driven Characterization of Real-World Experiments

Pairing machine-learned potentials with synchrotron, neutron, and high-temperature experiments to build models that reproduce measured structure.

Example workflow

How experiments become atomistic models

In prior Argonne work, high-temperature X-ray and neutron measurements were used to guide active learning for machine-learned interatomic potentials. The key idea remains central to our lab: start from the data, train selectively, and validate the model against the experiment before using it to predict what is difficult to measure directly.
